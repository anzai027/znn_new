A Variable-Gain Fixed-Time Convergent and
Robust ZNN Model for Image Fusion:
Design, Analysis, and Veriﬁcation
Lin Xiao , Xiangru Yan , Yongjun He , and Penglin Cao
Abstract—Image fusion can obtain the superior information
and reduce the noise in the source image by designing a speciﬁc
scheme. However, the noise in image fusion has been a difﬁcult
issue and hard to handle. In this article, a variable-gain ﬁxed-
time convergent and robust zeroing neural network (VFCR-ZNN)
model is proposed to ﬁgure out the image fusion problem and
the corresponding quadratic programming (QP) problem. In
contrast to the original zeroing neural network model, the VFCR-
ZNN model adopts a novel ﬁxed-time activation function and
a useful variable-gain parameter, which allows the VFCR-ZNN
model to converge faster in ﬁxed-time and realize noise immunity
under external disturbance. The detailed theory is provided
to support this point. Different numerical QP comparative
examples are carried out to effectively corroborate the rightness
of the theoretical analyses and the excellence of the VFCR-ZNN
model. Additionally, the quality of fused images acquired by the
VFCR-ZNN model is higher compared to existing state-of-the-art
models for image fusion. Furthermore, the VFCR-ZNN model
is successfully utilized in the repetitive motion of six-link robot
manipulator to demonstrate its signiﬁcant practical implications.
Index Terms —Fixed-time convergence, image fusion, noise
resistance, variable-gain parameter, zeroing neural network
(ZNN).
I. I NTRODUCTION
T
HE PROCESS of fusing image information about the
same target acquired from two or more different sensors
into a new image containing high-quality information is the
core problem of image fusion. Image fusion has a high-
research application in the ﬁelds of high-resolution processing
of images [1], [2], computer vision processing [3], [4], and
remote sensing [5], [6]. Nevertheless, in the process of images
acquisition and transmission, the images often be disturbed by
different noises due to the environment, imaging equipment
and human factors, which leads to the degradation of the

acquired images quality and affects the subsequent fused
images. Image fusion denoising means removing noise from
the source images while fusing the images. However, noise
processing in image fusion has been a challenging issue [7].
The image fusion denoising problem can be converted
into a quadratic programming (QP) problem, which can be
tackled by traditional numerical iterative methods and neural
network methods. While the traditional numerical algorithms
are usually based on serial processing, resulting in their
complexities are directly proportional to the dimensional cube,
and they have been proven to be inefﬁcient for dealing
with large-scale problems in practical applications [8], [9].I n
contrast, neural network-based methods have become powerful
and effective alternatives for solving various problems due to
their parallel distribution properties, easy implementation in
hardware, and high adaptability [10], [11], [12], [13]. Gradient
neural networks have superior computational ability, global
stability, and convergence in contrast to traditional numerical
methods [14], [15]. However, since gradient neural network
is scalar-based in its error design formulation, it can handle
static problems, but often fails to solve dynamic problems
accurately [16], [17]. Accounting for the mutability of the
coefﬁcients, most of the methods used to tackle static problems
exist certain lagged error when addressing dynamic problems,
which may cause tasks to fail. Therefore, Zhang et al.
suggested the zeroing neural network (ZNN) to tackle dynamic
problems. The ZNN has the ability to decay the lagged error
to zero by utilizing the time derivative of the coefﬁcient
matrix [18]. With this strength, the ZNN has caught ﬁre and
gained a lot of research and applications [19], [20], [21].
At present, domestic and international research on the
ZNN primarily focuses on its stability, convergence and
robustness. A newly designed sign-bi-power activation func-
tion (SBPAF) was reported, and the convergence time of
the ZNN model incorporating the SBPAF is experimentally
demonstrated to be bounded [22]. Some ﬁnite-time convergent
ZNN (FTZNN) models have emerged with signiﬁcantly faster
convergence [23]. Nevertheless, the convergence time of the
FTZNN models is uncertain and susceptible to the initial
state [24], [25]. Furthermore, it is highly challenging to control
the initial state in real applications [26], [27]. Therefore, it
is necessary to develop a ﬁxed-time convergent model that
remains unaffected by the initial state.
Additionally, there are always errors in the hardware real-
ization of ZNN, which are more complicated than the perfect

of the ZNN and yield diverse errors, like constant noises
caused by differential errors as well as model realization
errors [28], nonlinear time varying noises caused by envi-

conditions [28]. With the interference of these noises, the state

Most of the works concerning ZNN usually assume that
there is noise-free or that denoising is completed prior to the
calculation, so these ZNN models are largely noise-intolerant,
or relatively weakly noise-tolerant [29]. Therefore, an integral-
enhanced ZNN formulation was presented by Jin et al. [28],
which utilizes the integral control technique in control theory
for handling multiple noises.
There are relatively few studies regarding the utilization of
neural network methods for tackling image fusion problem.
In [30], a gradient neural network model was suggested to
address convex QP problems and was employed to tackle
the image fusion. Recently, ZNN models have been proposed
for image fusion [31], [32]. But none of them take into
account noise. Furthermore, the above mentioned ZNN models
primarily focus on the study of activation functions, while
the research on variable-gain parameters is relatively limited.
The ZNN models have shown better performance when the
variable-gain parameters are incorporated, which can usually
substantially increase the convergence speed [33].A sar e s u l t
of the aforementioned reasons, a variable-gain ﬁxed-time
convergent and robust ZNN (VFCR-ZNN) model is proposed
in this article.
The principal contributions of this article are summarized
as follows.
1) A VFCR-ZNN model with a novel nonlinear ﬁxed-
time activation function (NFTAF) and an outstanding
variable-gain parameter is put forward to tackle the
image fusion problem by solving the corresponding QP
problem.
2) The newly devised variable-gain parameter not only
improves the convergence speed of the VFCR-ZNN
model but also saves computational resources to a
certain extent.
3) The NFTAF enables the VFCR-ZNN model to possess
prominent ﬁxed-time convergence and splendid robust-
ness. Besides, the NFTAF renders the upper bound
of the convergence time of the VFCR-ZNN model
computable.
4) The simulation results demonstrate that the VFCR-
ZNN model achieves better-denoising performance,
compared to the existing ZNN models for image fusion.
Subsequently, the repetitive motion of the six-link robot
manipulator has demonstrated the practical value of the
VFCR-ZNN model.
Following is the structure of the remaining parts of this
article. The formulation of the image fusion problem and the
optimization process of the QP are described in Section II.
Section III illustrates the procedure of constructing the VFCR-
ZNN model, and presents the variable-gain parameter and the
NFTAF. Section IV analyzes the attributes of the VFCR-ZNN
model theoretically. Numerical simulations are conducted
using the VFCR-ZNN model in Section V. The VFCR-
ZNN model has been proven to be effective in both robot
manipulator repetitive motion and image fusion, as demon-
strated in Section VI. The primary consequences of this article
are concisely summed up in Section VII.
