GNN Model With Robust Finite-Time Convergence
for Time-Varying Systems of Linear Equations
Yinyan Zhang , Member, IEEE, Bolin Liao , and Guanggang Geng
Abstract—Dynamic neural networks are considered as an
effective method in the ﬁeld of scientiﬁc computing, among
which gradient neural networks (GNNs) are an efﬁcient method
for solving static problems. However, when solving dynamic
problems, the current GNNs are often subject to lagging
errors. In this article, we develop a ﬁnite-time convergent GNN
(FTCGNN) model for solving static and time-varying systems of
linear equations. Different from zeroing neural networks (ZNNs)
dedicated to time-varying problem solving, the FTCGNN model
has ﬁnite-time convergence regardless of the existence of time-
varying noises. Simulation results show that the FTCGNN model
is effective, among which the comparisons with existing GNNs
and ZNNs validate the advantages of the FTCGNN model.
Index Terms —Finite-time convergence, gradient neural
network (GNN), neurodynamics, systems of linear equations
(SLEs), time-varying problems.
I. I NTRODUCTION
D
YNAMIC neural networks (DNNs) are modeled as
ordinary differential equations. The beneﬁt of DNNs
lies in its convergence guarantee without off-line training
based on large amount of data. Such neural networks are
often with state feedbacks and are called recurrent neural
networks (RNNs). The most classical RNN should be the
Hopﬁeld neural network, which is named after its inventor
Hopﬁeld. Hopﬁeld and Tank [1] showed that DNNs can
efﬁciently solve the traveling-salesman problem that is NP-
complete. The early work of Hopﬁeld and Tank provides a
new perspective for solving scientiﬁc computing problems
by leveraging the advantages of DNNs. Technically, the
Hopﬁeld neural network relies on the minimization of an

for solving nonlinear constrained optimization problems, of

While the classical Hopﬁeld neural network targeting at
optimization problems, many novel DNN models have been
proposed to solve other types of scientiﬁc computing prob-
lems. Cichocki and Unbehauen [11] ﬁrst developed RNNs to
handle static systems of linear equations (SLEs). Wang and
Li [12] proposed a linear-function-activated RNN to address
linear equations, and the network has global asymptotic
convergence. It should be noted that the RNN developed
in [12] is a special case of the corresponding one proposed
in [11]. Essentially, the RNN proposed in [12] is a gradient
neural network (GNN) as its design leverages the energy
function’s gradient information. Zhang et al. [13] theoretically
analyzed the convergence behavior of the GNN in [12] with
different activation functions. Xia et al. [14] proposed globally
convergent RNNs to address systems of linear inequalities
and equations by converting the problem into an optimization
problem. Zhang et al. [15] proposed an RNN to handle
dynamic Sylvester equations, and the RNN is recognized as the
zeroing neural network (ZNN) in the existing literature. The
solution error of the ZNN has exponential convergence. Unlike
the Hopﬁeld neural network or GNN, the RNN proposed
in [15] is described by an implicit dynamic equation and its
design is not based on a non-negative energy function. Instead,
the RNN proposed in [15] relies on an error function that
can be matrix- or vector-valued and neural dynamics is then
designed for exponential error convergence. In terms of model
complexity, the ZNN is more complex than the Hopﬁeld neural
network, because the ZNN fully employs the time-derivative
information of all the matrices, vectors, and scalars involved
in the computing problems. The RNN proposed in [15] has
been adopted to solve many scientiﬁc computing problems,
including dynamic matrix inversion [16], dynamic general-
ized inversion [17], dynamic linear equation and inequality
systems [18], dynamic linear equations subject to bound
constraints [19], and time-varying linear equations [20].

Zhang et al.’s RNN [15] has been improved by mainly two
approaches. First, from the perspective of control theory, an
integration term is introduced to the original design formula,
by which the robustness of the neural network to noises
is improved [21], [22], [23], [24]. Second, an interesting
activation function is developed by Li et al. [25] such that the
RNN [15] converges in ﬁnite time when handling dynamic
Sylvester equations, i.e., the state variable of the RNN can
track the theoretical solution after certain time. Note that,
although ﬁnite-time stability has been widely investigated[26],
[27], [28], [29], [30], [31], most of the results focus on
the design methods of a ﬁnite-time controller for control
systems, and the extension of such results to the design of
neural networks of ﬁnite-time convergence when handling
scientiﬁc computing problems, such as the dynamic SLEs,
is not trivial. In the design of RNNs for solving scientiﬁc
computing problems, apart from the stability of the RNN
models, we must ensure that the state variable converges to
the corresponding theoretical solution. Following Li et al.’s
work [25], Xiao and Lu [32], Xiao et al. [33], Dai et al. [34],
and Lv et al. [35] proposed ﬁnite-time convergent RNNs for
solving various time-varying scientiﬁc computing problems.
Xiao et al. [36] also combined the above two improvements,
and further designed a robust ﬁnite-time convergent RNN for
solving dynamic linear equations. It should be noted that both
improvements make the model of the ZNN become more
complex.
It is widely recognized that GNNs have intrinsic dis-
advantages when they are adopted to solving time-varying
problems [37], [38], [39], [40]. For example, Xiao and Zhang
[40] showed that the traditional GNN is not favorable for
handling dynamic linear inequalities, and they argued that
this is due to the fact that GNN ignores time-derivative
information in the problem. In [41], for calculating the
inverse of a dynamic matrix, an adaptive coefﬁcient, including
time-derivative information, is introduced into the original
GNN. Owing to the adaptive coefﬁcient, the improved GNN
has asymptotic convergence for time-varying dynamic matrix
inversion, which is still not competent compared with the ZNN
of exponential convergence.
In this article, we provide a counter-intuition result for the
design of GNN. Speciﬁcally, we show that GNN can solve
time-varying SLEs in ﬁnite-time without knowing the exact
derivatives or partial derivatives about the SLEs. Evidently,
this result is more promising than the result of [41] in which
only asymptotic convergence of GNN for solving a time-
varying problem is achieved. Moreover, we also show that the
the proposed GNN has satisfactory robustness for the case
with noises. The major contributions in our work include the
following ones.
1) A uniﬁed GNN model is ﬁrst developed for solving
both static and dynamic SLEs with proven ﬁnite-time
convergence.
2) The proposed GNN model is of simpler struc-
ture compared with existing ZNN [24] dedicated
to solving time-varying SLEs under noises; unlike
ZNNs, such as [36], our GNN model does not
require knowing explicit formulas or exact values
of time-derivatives of variables involved in the
SLEs.
3) Our GNN model has ﬁnite-time convergence when
solving dynamic SLEs under additive bounded time-
varying noises by setting the design parameter.
We describe the considered problem and review the existing
GNN models for solving SLEs in Section II. Then, the design
and theoretical analysis of our GNN model are shown in
Section III. Simulation results and performance comparison
with existing ZNN and GNN models are shown in Section IV,
followed by conclusions and future works given in Section V.

A. Problem Description
An SLE can be described as follows:

are independent of time t, the SLE is static; otherwise, it is
called a time-varying or dynamic SLE.

a larger value of γ leads to faster convergence and better
robustness to noises.
Remark 4: Compared with NTZNN model (17), the advan-
tages of FTCGNN model (18) for solving time-varying SLEs
include the following aspects.
1) Compared with NTZNN model (17), the proposed
FTCGNN model (18) does not need the explicit formulas
or values of ˙A and ˙b. Instead, the proposed FTCGNN
model (18) only needs to know an upper bound of
∥˙x∗(t)∥2, which may be estimated in practical applica-
tions by using available prior knowledge.
2) Compared with NTZNN model (17), the proposed
FTCGNN model (18) does not need the integration oper-
ator to realize the robustness to noises. By comparing
(17) with (18), it can be readily found that the FTCGNN
model has a simpler structure.
3) Under bounded noises, the NTZNN model (17) cannot
achieve asymptotic convergence for solving time-
varying SLEs, while the proposed FTCGNN model (18)
can achieve ﬁnite-time convergence under mild condi-
tions.
Remark 5: The limitation of the proposed method lies in the
requirement on knowing the upper bound of ∥˙x
∗(t)∥2, i.e., the
Euclidian norm of the time derivative of the theoretical solu-
tion to the time-varying SLE. According to Theorems 2 and 3,
to ensure ﬁnite-time convergence of the proposed FTCGNN
model, γ should be larger than a value that depends on the
upper bound of ∥˙x
∗(t)∥2. In practice, for some applications,
we can determine the upper bound of ∥˙x∗(t)∥2 based on the
physical meaning of ˙x∗. For example, for the manipulator
application shown in Section IV-E of this article, we have prior
knowledge about the maximum joint acceleration ¨θ for a given
physical manipulator, and we can use this prior knowledge
to get an upper bound of ∥˙x∗(t)∥2. For the case that such
prior knowledge is not available, we can set the value of γ
to be sufﬁciently large such that the condition about γ can
be satisﬁed. We can determine whether the setting of γ is
suitable by monitoring the convergence of the residual error
∥A(t)x(t) − b(t)∥2.
According to Theorems 1–3, the proposed FTCGNN
method is not sensitive to initial conditions, i.e., global
convergence is theoretically guaranteed given that the model
parameters are properly set. Such a property is desirable for
practical applications.

V. C ONCLUSION
In this article, a FTCGNN model has been proposed to
solve both static and time-varying SLEs. Without knowing
the explicit formulas or exact values of derivative information
about time-varying SLEs, under mild conditions, the state
variable of the proposed GNN has ﬁnite-time convergence
to the corresponding theoretical solution regardless of the
existence of time-varying noises. The effectiveness and supe-
riority of the proposed GNN model have been validated
by simulation results, including comparisons with the state-
of-the-art methods for time-varying problem solving. An
application to the kinematic control of a UR5 manipulator
has also substantiated the potential impact of the proposed
model. Nonlinear control problems are widely encountered
in engineering applications. There have been some works
that convert nonlinear control problems into time-varying
optimization problems, such as [51], [52], and [53].A sa
result, it is possible to extend the current method to deal with
nonlinear control problems by converting the problem into
a time-varying optimization problem. It would be interesting
to investigate the extension of the proposed model to deal
with time-varying optimization problems for nonlinear control.
Given that matrix A is uncertain, the accurate solution of SLEs
cannot be found. The extension of the proposed method for
the case that matrix A of SLEs is uncertain will be our future
work.
